# How your finals are graded

Every piece of your finals has a short rubric. Here they all are, so you know
exactly what earns the marks. The report, documentation, journal, and project
are read and graded with written feedback; the presentation is graded by hand.

## Project Increment Report (20 points, weeks 1 and 2)

| Criterion | Max | What earns full marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visible progress | 8 | Real work this week, backed by your commits. In week 2, the report is clear about what changed since week 1. |
| Changes described | 6 | You state clearly what you added, changed, or fixed, and why. |
| Honesty about blockers and what remains | 6 | You name what broke, what you are stuck on, and what is left. |

## Documentation Update (15 points, weeks 1 and 2)

See `documentation-guide.md` for what each section must contain. Week 2 adds one
row for your security checklist, and the other three rows make room for it, so
the total stays at 15 in both weeks.

### Week 1

| Criterion | Max | What earns full marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setup and run steps | 6 | A reader can follow your instructions to run the app from nothing. |
| Feature and usage docs | 5 | The docs explain what the app does and how to use it, matching the real code. |
| Screenshots and polish | 4 | Screenshots show the app running, and the writing is clear. |

### Week 2

| Criterion | Max | What earns full marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setup and run steps | 5 | A reader can follow your instructions to run the app from nothing. |
| Feature and usage docs | 4 | The docs explain what the app does and how to use it, matching the real code. |
| Screenshots and polish | 3 | Screenshots show the app running, and the writing is clear. |
| Security checklist | 3 | `SECURITY-CHECKLIST.md` is filled in from the template in this unit, every row answered Yes, No or N/A with one line of evidence, and every N/A carrying its reason. Blank rows, and answers your repository contradicts, score nothing. |

## Reflection Journal (15 points, weeks 1 and 2)

| Criterion | Max | What earns full marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concrete, not vague | 8 | Specific things you actually did and specific problems you actually hit. |
| Honest self-assessment | 4 | Honest about what went well, what did not, and what you would change. |
| Consistency | 3 | The entry covers the goal, the work, the blockers, and the lesson. |

## Final Project (100 points, week 3, the exam)

| Criterion | Max | What earns full marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Builds and runs | 20 | The server starts, connects to its database, and the endpoints respond. |
| API design and correctness | 20 | Routes are sensible and RESTful, do what they should, and return the right status codes. |
| Database schema and queries | 20 | The schema models the problem well, queries are correct, input is handled safely. |
| Error handling and validation | 15 | Bad input and failures are handled gracefully. |
| Scope and completeness | 15 | Delivers what the proposal set out, at a real, usable level. |
| Code quality | 10 | Readable, sensibly organised, no dead or copy-pasted filler. |

## Builds Full-Stack JavaScript and AI (100 points, week 3, the badge)

Graded from `AI-USAGE.md` in your project repository and from your README. See
the `finals-badge` unit for the full rubric.

| Criterion | Max | What earns full marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How I used AI | 35 | Six or more entries, each with the tool, the request, what you kept or changed, and a commit link. |
| Where the AI got it wrong | 25 | Three real cases, with the output, the problem, the fix, and a commit link. |
| Who wrote what | 30 | Your own parts named with file and commit, explained in your own words, plus one AI-written piece explained as clearly. |
| README credit | 10 | A badge, a line naming the assistant, and a link to `AI-USAGE.md`. |

The **badge is awarded at 75 or above**. Being honest about heavy AI use does not
cost you marks; claiming the AI was never wrong does.

## Presentation (100 points, week 3)

| Part | Max | What earns full marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Video (3 to 5 min, public Google Drive link) | 60 | You walk through the app and the code and explain your choices, on camera and in your own voice. Not production polish. |
| AI segment, inside the video | included above | Two to three minutes on how you used AI to build this and what you wrote yourselves. Required; a video without it loses marks from the video row. |
| Slides | 25 | Problem, demo, tech, challenges, and what is next. |
| Square image (1080 by 1080) | 15 | Project title, your name, and one screenshot or logo. |

Missing one part costs that part's points, not the whole grade.
